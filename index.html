Salon Brand Identity (Self-initiated project): Roselle - An art of beauty

Roselle is a concept brand identity for a premium
beauty and spa studio, built around the idea that
self-care should feel soft, indulgent, and quietly
luxurious rather than clinical. The project imagines
a complete identity for a modern spa targeting clients
who see their beauty routine as a ritual,
not a chore.

Beauty and spa branding is a crowded, visually similar space — soft pinks, floral motifs, and script
logotypes are everywhere. The challenge was building an identity that used that familiar emotional
language (softness, elegance, femininity) without feeling generic or interchangeable with every other
spa brand on Pinterest.

Approach

  1. Explored the brand’s core feeling first — "quiet luxury" and
    "bloom" — before touching any logo sketches, to keep every
    later decision anchored to a single emotional idea.
  2. Developed a hand-lettered logotype paired with a rose icon,
    using the rose as a recurring visual motif across the identity
    rather than a one-off logo element.
  3. Built a soft, warm color palette (Peach Monochrome) grounded
    in real spa photography, so the palette would feel authentic to
    the space rather than arbitrary.
  4. Extended the identity into a full social media kit — grid layout,
    story templates, and sample posts — so the brand could be
    tested in the environment it would actually live in day to day.

Process

While the process of creating a proper logo (shown on the right side) there are also some logos
which are partially developed and then rejected, in this rejected logo (on the left side) I found that
its may be seems like a simple text with a decorative flower and I wanted to craft the original logo
with some extra-touch in the logo as I wanted to make the logo stand out differently which stays
in the viwers mind for hours.

Solution

The final identity centers on a hand-lettered "Roselle" logotype
and a delicate rose icon, supported by a Peach Monochrome
color system and soft script/serif typography. The system was
applied across business stationery, a styled Instagram grid, and
story templates — giving the brand a consistent, recognizable feel
whether a client encounters it on a business card or while scrolling
their phone.

Logistics Brand Identity (Self-initiated project): Meridian Express Logistics

Meridian Express Logistics is a concept brand identity
for a logistics and freight company, built to feel
dependable, fast, and professional across every
touchpoint a logistics brand actually needs — from
a driver’s uniform to an invoice template.

Logistics branding lives across an unusually wide range of real-world applications at once — vehicles,
uniforms, paperwork, and digital channels — all of which need to stay instantly recognizable at different
scales and in different environments, from a small invoice line to the side of a moving truck.

Approach

  1. Built the identity around a bold, angular "M" monogram mark,
    designed to stay legible at both large scale (vehicle livery) and
    small scale (favicon, stamp).
  2. Chose a navy-and-orange palette — navy for trust and stability,
    orange for speed and urgency — a combination deliberately
    suited to the logistics sector’s core promises.
  3. Designed the identity outward from the logo across every real
    operational touchpoint: uniforms (cap, polo, formal pants), van
    and truck livery, a company stamp, letterhead, and an invoice
    template.
  4. Extended the system into marketing and social applications — a
    social banner, holiday notice templates, and post/story formats —
    so the brand felt consistent across both back-office documents
    and public-facing content.

Process

While the process of creating a proper logo (shown on the right side) there are also some logos which are
partially developed and then rejected, in this rejected logo (on the left side) I found that it may seem
like a copy or a direct inspiration of someone’s logo and as I wanted to craft the original logo with some
extra-touch in the logo I chose the other one as that looks original and memorable.

Solution

The final system is a full brand identity built around the
navy-and-orange "M" monogram, consistently applied across uniforms,
vehicle livery, stationery, an invoice template, and social media
content. The breadth of applications was intentional — the goal
was to prove the identity could hold up as a real operational
system, not just a logo on a mockup.
